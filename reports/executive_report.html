Experiment Readout

Progress Gate Placement

Cookie Cats · Mobile Game
n = 90,189 players
Gate 30 (control) vs. Gate 40 (treatment)
Recommendation: do not ship

Moving the first progress gate from level 30 to level 40 was hypothesized to improve retention by keeping players in the core loop longer before friction. The data shows the opposite: Day‑7 retention drops significantly, with no offsetting engagement gain.

Secondary & Guardrail Metrics

The drop is real, and it isn't costing engagement

Day‑1 retention moved the same direction (44.82% → 44.23%) but didn't clear the multiple‑comparison‑adjusted significance bar — this is a longer‑horizon retention story, not a first‑impression one. Engagement (median game rounds played) was statistically unchanged between arms (17 vs. 16), confirmed after excluding extreme outlier accounts — so the retention loss in the treatment group isn't offset by a corresponding engagement gain.

    Used instead of a t-test — engagement counts are heavily right‑skewed with extreme outliers, violating the normality assumption a t-test needs.

    At the achieved sample size, the test could detect effects as small as 0.74pp at 80% power — smaller than the pre-registered 1.0pp MDE, confirming the null results aren't just underpowered noise.

    Complete
Caveats

The observed effect (0.82pp) is statistically significant but sits under the pre-registered minimum effect size (1.0pp) that was decided in advance to be worth acting on — a genuine but modest signal, not overstated here. The minor sample imbalance (p = 0.0086 on the SRM check) is within accepted tolerance but has been flagged to the team owning randomization.
